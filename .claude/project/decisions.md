# Decision Log (ADRs)

Append-only record of significant decisions. Newest first. Format: date · decision · why.

## 2026-08-10 (e) · Auto-apply is on-board, and nothing is sent without a review
New `portal_applications` table + `ApplicationService`. `PreviewAsync` does all the work (match, letters, duplicate detection) and stores nothing; `SubmitAsync` only persists what came back from a human. Bulk apply is capped at 25 postings. **Why:** applying is outward-facing and cannot be undone from here, so the split is where consent lives, not a UI nicety — bulk apply without it is a button that writes to strangers on the candidate's behalf. A fit floor with no cap is how "apply to anything above 40%" reaches a hundred employers off one sentence. External ATS automation was deliberately not built: it needs per-vendor form handling and credentials, breaks on markup changes, and mass submission violates most job boards' terms; the contracts are shaped so an assisted-external path (prepare, review, human submits) can be added without redoing this.

## 2026-08-10 (e) · The fit snapshot is frozen at submission
Score, band, sub-scores and per-skill verdicts are copied onto the application row rather than re-derived on read. **Why:** a recruiter opening an application months later must see what the candidate saw when they applied. Re-scoring would move the number as models, thresholds and the posting itself change, and the applicant would be judged against a bar that did not exist when they applied. The row also records whether the score came from real semantic matching, because a 46% from the hashing fallback is not the same claim as a 46% that means something.

## 2026-08-10 (e) · Duplicate applications are prevented by the database, keyed on the document
Unique index on `(JobId, ResumeContentHash)`, not `(JobId, ResumeId)`. **Why:** a service-level check cannot be the guarantee — two clicks race, and a candidate who re-uploads the same CV gets a fresh `portal_resumes` row and applies again in good faith. Keying on the content hash makes the second attempt impossible whichever row it came from. `SubmitAsync` catches the collision and falls back to per-row inserts so one duplicate cannot lose a batch.

## 2026-08-10 (e) · The cover letter is grounded twice, and contact details never come from the model
The model is given only the candidate's evidenced skills, title and years, and the posting's requirements — never the resume text to embroider. Any letter that mentions a skill scored *Missing*, or that contains an email or a 9+ digit number, is discarded for the deterministic one. **Why:** this is the most dangerous text the portal produces — everything else the model writes describes a result the candidate can check, but this goes to an employer under their name. The Missing-skill check is deliberately strict enough to reject even an honest "keen to learn X", because separating that from "experienced in X" is prose comprehension and being wrong costs an interview. The contact check was added because a test caught the gap: the prompt forbade addresses but nothing enforced it, and an invented address routes an employer's reply into a void.

## 2026-08-10 (d) · The assistant navigates the app deterministically, against the route registry
`components/layout/navIntent.ts` resolves "take me to X" by scoring the sentence against `NAV_GROUPS` — the same registry the sidebar renders — and returns navigate / describe / ambiguous / unknown. The LLM is not consulted. **Why:** this is the same invariant the portal already holds — *the model never supplies a fact, and never supplies a requirement*. A model asked "which page do they want?" will confidently answer "the Settings page" for an app with no such page, and the user then hunts for something that was never built. Scoring against the registry makes "sorry, there is no such page" a fact about the application. Route vocabulary (`description` + `keywords`) lives **on the route** in `navConfig.ts` rather than in a table beside the assistant, because a page and the words that reach it are one fact; splitting them is how a renamed page quietly stops answering to anything. `navConfig.test.ts` fails if a route is added without them, or becomes unreachable by its own name.

## 2026-08-10 (d) · The navigator declines by default
`resolveNavigation` returns null unless the sentence really reads as navigation, and the evidence required scales with how plainly it asked: a strong cue ("take me to…", "the X page") claims the sentence outright; a weak cue ("show/where/find") needs the page's name, a group name or a solid keyword hit; a bare question needs a full page or group name. **Why:** everything the navigator claims is taken away from the job-portal conversation. "Show me remote roles" is a search, not a request for the Job Board page, and "why is this candidate a 46% fit" was being claimed by Candidate Ranking on the word "candidate" — a question about a score silently becoming navigation. Both are regression-tested. Ambiguity is answered with a question rather than a guess, because the user cannot tell a wrong guess from the app not having what they wanted.

## 2026-08-10 (c) · One website: the portal UI folds into the recruiter SPA, chat becomes a popup
`job_portal/frontend` is retired. Its three pages moved into `ai_hiring_platform/frontend/src/portal/`: the chat is now a floating popup mounted once by the app shell (`ChatWidget`), and Board / Post a Job are sections in the existing sidebar. The .NET API is untouched and still a separate process on :5290. **Why:** the user wanted one navigable product rather than two sites, with the assistant reachable from every page. Mounting the popup in the shell — not on a page — is what makes it a popup: closing it hides the panel without ending the conversation or dropping the socket, and navigating between sections cannot tear the hub down. State lives in `PortalState` above the page switch for the same reason, and because Copilot actions must be able to drive a board the user is not currently looking at. Kept in one quarantined folder mirroring the .NET contract, so the two backends' contracts never merge into one file (Golden Rule 9 applies per backend).

## 2026-08-10 (c) · SignalR assemblies come from the shared framework, never from a PackageReference
Removed `Microsoft.AspNetCore.SignalR.Common` 10.0.10 from a `net9.0` project. **Why:** it overrode one assembly of the ASP.NET Core 9 shared framework with a build for a different framework version; the framework's `JsonHubProtocol` and that 10.x `IInvocationBinder` disagree, so parsing the FIRST hub message threw `MissingMethodException`. The handshake succeeded and every send then killed the connection — the chat had never worked. The browser saw only "Connection closed with an error", and nothing was logged above Warning, so the failure pointed at the client. `Microsoft.NET.Sdk.Web` already supplies every SignalR server type; the pin was never needed.

## 2026-08-10 (c) · The hub connection is lazy, and StrictMode-safe
`useChatHub` takes an `enabled` flag and opens no socket until the assistant is first opened; teardown waits for `start()` to settle before calling `stop()`, and a `disposed` guard stops a torn-down connection reporting state. **Why:** (1) a recruiter who never opens the assistant should not have a socket opened to a .NET service they may not be running, now that the widget is mounted app-wide. (2) StrictMode double-invokes the effect on one component instance, so stopping synchronously aborted the first connection mid-negotiate ("stopped during negotiation") and its `onclose` — sharing the same `setState` as the live connection — then overwrote 'connected' with 'disconnected', showing a working socket as offline. Covered by `useChatHub.test.tsx`.

## 2026-08-10 · Job Portal is a separate .NET 9 + React stack under `job_portal/`
Candidate-facing portal (job board + resume chat) built as its own solution and its own Vite app on :5290/:5174, sharing the Python platform's SQLite file. **Why:** the user asked for this feature as a distinct tech stack; it also keeps Golden Rule 6 intact — the FastAPI backend has no knowledge of it and nothing in it imports Python code.

## 2026-08-10 · Shared database = one file, disjoint table sets, raw idempotent DDL
Portal tables are all prefixed `portal_`; `job_descriptions`/`resumes` are mapped read-only and never written or migrated from .NET. Created with `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` (`PortalSchemaInitializer`), not EF migrations. **Why:** an EF migrations history table would assert ownership over a schema SQLAlchemy owns, and a stray `dotnet ef database update` could then "fix" the recruiter platform's tables; `EnsureCreated` does nothing once the file has any table, so on a SQLAlchemy-created database the portal's tables would never appear.

## 2026-08-10 · Portal resumes are NOT written into the Python `resumes` table
Candidate uploads land in `portal_resumes`. **Why:** the Python table is the recruiter's screening pool; portal uploads are walk-in job seekers. Merging them would silently enrol every applicant into the recruiter's candidate pool.

## 2026-08-10 · The model supplies neither facts nor requirements (portal)
Extraction runs deterministically first and the LLM only fills gaps; every skill it proposes must appear verbatim in the source document or it is dropped. Fit scores, comparison tables and recruiter notes are composed in C# from the scored result. **Why:** same invariant as the recruiter chatbot — a hallucinated *requirement* silently changes the question before retrieval runs, and becomes a real gap that sends a candidate to learn something no employer asked for.

## 2026-08-10 · Deterministic embedder is a declared floor, not a silent substitute
With no embedding endpoint the portal uses a hashed word+trigram embedder, and the health endpoint and the chat both say matching is lexical. **Why:** the fallback cannot know "React" answers "frontend framework"; presenting lexical results as semantic ones makes an unexplainable absence look like a considered judgement.

## 2026-08-06 · Match Score replaces the 5-dimension overall score
`overall_score` is now the nine-parameter Match Score (Skill 20, Experience 15, Technology 14, Designation 14, Industry 9, Education 8, Location 8, Availability 7, Freshness 5), reported to one decimal with bands 85/70/50. New modules: `match_scoring_service`, `job_profile_extractor` (structured JD facts, which did not exist — the JD was parsed only into requirement strings), `candidate_facets_service`, `text_matching`. Extraction sits in Agent 1 (evidence), scoring in Agent 2 (judgement). The five legacy sub-scores remain computed and displayed as "Evidence Sub-scores" because the strengths/weaknesses/roadmap/questions are reasoned from them. Full spec in `docs/Match_Score.md`. **Why:** an agreed cross-team specification — three people scoring the same candidate must get the same number, so the weights, the neutral values and the one-decimal precision are a contract, not a preference. Two rules make it defensible: a requirement the JD never stated scores neutrally (otherwise the score measures JD verbosity, not candidate quality), and claimed skills are scored separately from practised ones (Skill vs Technology), which is the judgement collapsing them into one number destroys.

## 2026-08-06 · One set of score thresholds across the product
Dashboard buckets moved 80/60 → 70/50, frontend colours 75/60 → 70/50, recruiter recommendation gained a fourth band, rejection drafts now written only below 50. All derive from `MATCH_SCORE_BANDS`. **Why:** three independent threshold sets existed; a dashboard reading "Rejected" beside a report reading "Strong fit" presents two opinions as one fact. A rejection draft for a Moderate fit contradicted the same report's advice to interview.

## 2026-08-06 · Skills sections are read as lists, not by token shape
`candidate_facets_service.declared_skills` splits a Skills section on its separators; `technical_terms` (shape-based: acronym, internal caps, digit, tech punctuation) still handles prose. **Why:** shape-based extraction is right for prose, where a technology must announce itself among English, and wrong for a list, where every comma-separated item is a skill by construction. It also had a specific failure: a misspelling like `Kubernets` has no technical shape, so it was never extracted, and the spec's partial-credit-for-typos rule could never fire on the one place typos actually live.

## 2026-08-21 · Prompt Lab — a prompt is measured, not judged by another LLM
New section (`pages/PromptLab`, `/api/v1/prompt-lab`) that runs a system prompt over a fixed set of turns and scores it clause by clause. The LLM only GENERATES the answer under test; every verdict on that answer is deterministic (`services/ai/prompt_rules_service.py` — 14 rules: line/character limits, markdown-vs-plain URLs, link policy per turn, repeated links, URL and number grounding, years-only-when-verified, unrequested lists, context skills + skills URL, instruction leak, per-case required/forbidden strings). A rule with nothing to say about a turn returns N/A and is excluded from the denominator, so a thin case set cannot flatter a bad prompt. **Why:** an LLM judge scores the same answer differently on a rerun, which is exactly what a regression suite cannot tolerate (CLAUDE.md §4). Expectations live on the CASE, not the prompt, so the same suite grades a rewritten prompt without being rewritten. Runs are stored with the prompt fingerprint AND the model, because a history that cannot tell a prompt edit from a model swap is noise. The lab is a test harness, not a third runtime agent — it never joins `hiring_workflow.py`.

## 2026-07-18 · Candidate profile & seniority fit (F3)
Added `services/ai/profile_service.py` — deterministic extraction of name/title/total-years from the parsed resume text + seniority-band classification, compared against the JD's stated "N+ years" to yield Below/Meets/Exceeds/Unknown. Agent 1 computes it and attaches `candidate_profile` to the evidence; evaluation carries it onto `HiringReport.candidate_profile` (both paths). Optional/nullable for backward-compat. **Why:** pure skill-fit ignores "who is this and are they senior enough" — a core recruiter question. Kept deterministic (regex/heuristics, no hardcoded names) on the evidence side; the LLM never sees the raw resume (Golden Rule 2). 120 tests pass. Frontend handoff + example payload updated. Held next: chunking, hybrid, multi-candidate ranking.

## 2026-07-18 · Provider-agnostic LLM engine + grounded reasoning core (F1)
Reasoning must never be tied to a vendor/model. Added `services/ai/llm_service.py` — one factory driven by `LLM_PROVIDER` + `LLM_MODEL` (env), supporting openai / anthropic / google-gemini (aliases: gemini→google, claude→anthropic), lazy per-provider imports, per-provider default models, returns None → deterministic fallback. Installed `llama-index-llms-google-genai`; config reads `GOOGLE_API_KEY` or `GEMINI_API_KEY`. Removed the hardcoded provider imports + stale `claude-3-5-sonnet-20240620` id from `evaluation_service`. Rebuilt the LLM path into testable helpers: `_build_reasoning_prompt` (grounded, per-requirement evidence + must/nice importance, evidence-only), `_extract_json` (fence/prose-tolerant), `_finalize_llm_report` (deterministic ownership: quality+authenticity+weighted coverage+importance computed in code, overall recombined). Tested with a fake LLM (no network). **Why:** user directive — avoid vendor lock-in, current key is Gemini Pro; and the mock was the only working "reasoning". Golden Rule 2 preserved (LLM sees only retrieved evidence). Default model chosen fully config-driven (LLM_MODEL). F3 (candidate profile/seniority) deferred. 115 tests pass.

## 2026-07-18 · Requirement prioritization (must-have vs nice-to-have) + weighted coverage (F2)
Every requirement was scored with equal weight, so missing a nice-to-have hurt as much as missing a must-have. Added deterministic `classify_priorities(jd_text, requirements)` in the extractor: JD priority headers ("Nice to have:", "Requirements:") set a running context, inline cues ("a plus", "required") override it, default is must-have. Agent 1 attaches `importance`+`weight` to each evidence item; `coverage_score` is now importance-weighted (`_weighted_coverage`) in both mock and LLM paths; `RequirementFit` gained optional `importance`/`weight`; missing must-haves are surfaced first in recruiter weaknesses. Defaults (weight 1.0) keep all prior behavior/tests identical. **Why:** recruiter relevance + it is the enabler F1 (grounded reasoning) and future candidate ranking both depend on. Chosen order was F2→F1. Deterministic baseline now; F1 adds an LLM refinement pass over it. 107 tests pass.

## 2026-07-18 · Generalized (deterministic) JD requirement extraction + generic tech-specificity
`jd_requirement_extractor` was pure keyword lookup against `TECH_TAXONOMY` — any skill off that ~100-term list was invisible, so evaluation was silently capped to known tech. Rebuilt as two deterministic layers: (1) taxonomy precision match, (2) grammar-cue extraction that pulls unknown tools, soft skills, domain phrases, and seniority ("5+ years") straight from the JD text (bullets / cue lines / requirement blocks), with bounded, deduped, noise-filtered phrases. Also replaced the second hardcoded keyword regex in `retrieval_service` (tech-specificity signal) with a generic morphology+taxonomy signal. **Why:** the vision explicitly rejects keyword matching and Golden Rules 4–5 demand algorithms-over-examples that generalize to any JD. Kept extraction deterministic (not LLM) because the JD is a raw document and Golden Rule 2 bars the LLM from raw docs. Verified: retrieval math is genuine cosine (BGE normalized + FAISS IndexFlatIP — probe-confirmed, no bug). 101 tests pass.

## 2026-08-05 · One shared embedding store replaces two parallel index layers
`embedding_store.py` owns parsing, chunking and vectors for the whole platform. Documents are parsed ONCE into a model-independent chunk set (`documents.db`); each model holds its own `vectors-<model>.faiss` + `rows-<model>.json` over that same set. `corpus_index_service` and the evaluation agent are now thin readers of it, and `vector_store_service`'s per-resume indexes are no longer built. **Why:** the platform embedded every resume twice with BGE (once per-resume for Analysis/Ranking, once pool-wide for the chatbot), and the per-resume index hardcoded `d = 384`, so the model selector did nothing outside the chatbot. A third model on that shape would have meant six embedding passes per resume and three ways to diverge. Sharing one chunk set is also what makes a model comparison meaningful — every model is asked to index exactly the same text.

## 2026-08-05 · FAISS, not a vector database
Kept FAISS flat indexes rather than adopting Qdrant. **Why:** measured, not assumed — all three models together are 384+768+1024 = 2176 floats per chunk, so the current pool is ~78 MB total and a flat search is 2-5 ms. A server would add deployment surface (the platform ships as one container) for data that fits in RAM many times over. The store is a single module behind a narrow interface (`load_index`/`search`/`index_model`), so a Qdrant adapter is a contained addition if the team standardises on it; nothing above that interface would change.

## 2026-08-05 · Indexing is explicit and per model
No implicit embedding at upload. `POST /embeddings/index` takes a list of models and runs them sequentially on a worker thread with polled progress; `/embeddings/coverage` reports per-model resume and chunk counts plus the intersection. **Why:** the implicit path is exactly what failed — 300 resumes uploaded while the RunPod endpoint was down entered the BGE index and nothing else, and no screen said so, so two models were answering questions about different pools. Explicit selection also means an outage is workable: index the two local models, carry on, and fill in the remote one later while the UI shows it is behind. Sequential rather than parallel because the models contend for CPU and a 0.64 GB model beside another is how the ONNX allocator dies.

## 2026-08-05 · Third engine: mxbai-embed-large-v1, local, with a calibrated floor
Added as `mxbai` (1024 dims) via FastEmbed/ONNX, implemented like BGE rather than behind the GPU endpoint, so it can index with the network down. Its similarity floor (0.537) was measured to match BGE's selectivity at 0.62 on 279 chunks of this project's real resumes, and its batch size is pinned to 8. **Why:** a shared cosine threshold across models rewards whichever model scores higher in absolute terms, which says nothing about ranking quality — equal selectivity is what makes the three-way comparison about the model. The batch size is a correctness setting, not tuning: the library default of 256 requested a 1.15 GB activation buffer and crashed, and throughput is flat from 8 to 64 regardless (~1.9 chunks/s on CPU).

## 2026-08-05 · Model Lab: comparison over an equal population, retrieval first
A dedicated page and `POST /embeddings/compare`. Every selected model answers the same question; `fair_mode` confines them all to the resumes they have ALL indexed; the deterministic retrieval answer is always shown and the LLM summary is opt-in. Reports top-1 unanimity and pairwise Jaccard overlap. **Why:** without an equal population a model wins by having seen more resumes, which measures indexing coverage rather than retrieval. Without the deterministic answer the LLM's run-to-run wording variance hides the very difference being measured. And since a resume pool has no labelled ground truth, the honest metric is agreement, not accuracy — unanimity means the model choice is not currently buying anything, and divergence is where a human should look.

## 2026-08-05 · The working set — uploading and embedding are separate decisions
`documents.db` is now treated as an explicit **working set** rather than a mirror of the resumes table: the subset parsed and chunked, and therefore the only subset that can be embedded or searched. New `services/document_manager.py` lists it, adds to it, removes from it (reversible — files and rows survive) and deletes permanently; `api/v1/routers/documents.py` exposes it; `pages/Documents` is the screen. Uploads of `AUTO_INDEX_MAX_BATCH` files or fewer still auto-index; larger batches are stored and left out until selected. Startup catches models up on the existing working set with `expand=False` and never grows it. **Why:** with 300+ CVs, embedding everything on arrival spends ~40 minutes of CPU (mxbai at ~1.9 chunks/s) deciding on the recruiter's behalf that a whole client dump is worth indexing. Which CVs are worth that cost is a judgement only they can make. Removal is split in two because taking an expensive model off a candidate must not mean losing the candidate.

## 2026-08-05 · Gallery-gesture multi-select rather than checkboxes
`hooks/useGallerySelection` implements click / ctrl-click / shift-click / press-and-drag-to-sweep / Ctrl+A / Esc over an ordered id list, and clears ids that leave the visible list. Drag is RANGE-based from an anchor, not a geometric rubber-band. **Why:** picking 60 resumes out of 300 with one checkbox per row is 60 precise clicks and nobody does it twice; these gestures are already in the user's fingers from every photo gallery. Range-from-anchor behaves correctly whatever the layout does, whereas a marquee rectangle has to be re-taught the layout and breaks when rows reflow.

## 2026-08-05 · The corpus decides what its own words mean (`chat_lexicon`)
A word's meaning is resolved against the indexed pool, not against a harvested keyword list. For every term the lexicon measures, over the corpus text, the fraction of occurrences written in plain lowercase and the share of resumes it appears in; a term that reads as ordinary English *in this pool* can never become a hard search requirement. Places are harvested from address-shaped lines (postal code / address label / contact tail) so the pool is its own gazetteer, and people are indexed by token SET so "naveen k" and "K Naveen" reach the same person. Resumes whose name extraction failed carry a `Candidate #N` placeholder and are excluded from name matching entirely. **Why:** every serious retrieval failure traced to one root cause — a query word assigned a meaning the corpus does not support. "now" and "point" became required skills (a search matched all 306 resumes; a candidate's score fell 88%→56%); the literal word "candidate" matched the placeholder names and turned a skill search into a person lookup, silently discarding the Java requirement. No fixed lists beyond English *function words* and the grammar of an address, so Golden Rule 4 holds.

## 2026-08-05 · Four answer shapes: search · fact · explanation · question-back
`QueryKind` gains ATTRIBUTE and META; `chat_service` routes each to its own path. A fact question is answered from that candidate's own chunks (`search_within_resume` reconstructs their vectors from the flat index), with "the resume doesn't state that" as a legitimate outcome. An explanation is answered from the STORED previous result and never re-runs retrieval. Facet sets are kept separate rather than intersected, so an empty conjunction reports which half failed with counts and offers measured relaxations. **Why:** one shape for every question meant "where is he from" was refused as off-topic, a two-constraint question quietly dropped a constraint and answered with an unrelated person, and defending a ranking re-scored the candidate mid-defence. Re-running retrieval to justify a number is how 88% became 56% inside an explanation of the 88%.

## 2026-08-05 · The LLM labels spans; it never assigns meaning (`chat_llm_parser`)
When the deterministic parser is stuck, the LLM is asked only to mark up the recruiter's sentence — this span is a person, this is a place, this names a technology. Every span must appear VERBATIM in the message and then resolve through `chat_lexicon`; validated spans are rebuilt into a query and re-parsed, so one code path decides the final intent. **Why:** asking a model "what skills are being requested?" reproduces the old failure less predictably — an 8B model will report `now` as a skill or invent a candidate. A hallucinated *requirement* silently changes the question before retrieval, which is as damaging as a hallucinated answer. Worst case is a fall back to the deterministic reading, so this is safe on a small local model.

## 2026-08-05 · Guardrail scope is a property of the conversation
`check_query` takes `conversation_has_subject`. A pronoun-only message is in scope once an earlier turn established who is being discussed. **Why:** "where is he from" carries no hiring noun and was blocked as off-topic — immediately after "tell me about Naveen" it is the most natural question a recruiter could ask. Judging a message in isolation made the assistant refuse its own follow-ups.

## 2026-07-18 · Deterministic authenticity / keyword-stuffing detection + real quality_score
New `services/ai/authenticity_service.py` computes credibility from evidence sections: a skill is *demonstrated* only if it has Experience/Projects evidence; skills appearing solely in Skills/Summary are *over-claimed* (keyword stuffing). Emits `AuthenticityAssessment` (credibility_score, keyword_stuffing_risk, over_claimed_skills, corroboration_ratio, explanation) on `HiringReport` and replaces the hardcoded `quality_score = 85` with a corroboration+depth blend. Computed on **both** the LLM and mock paths — never delegated to the LLM. Field is `Optional` for backward-compat with pre-existing stored reports. **Why:** the vision explicitly demands "is the candidate exaggerating / keyword stuffing?"; the hardcoded 85 violated algorithms-over-examples. Fits the two-agent model as a deterministic service Agent 2 consumes evidence-first — no new agent, fully explainable.

## 2026-07-17 · Parallel dev via git worktrees (2 + main hub)
Two worktrees (`cc2-backend` → `dev/backend`, `cc2-frontend` → `dev/frontend`) plus the main worktree as integration/QA/push hub. QA is on-demand in the main worktree, not a 3rd permanent worktree. **Why:** the main worktree already has the full env; a dedicated QA worktree would add duplication and constant merges. Fewer moving parts, faster for a 2-day sprint.

## 2026-07-17 · Backend worktrees reuse the main `.venv`
Backend sessions run pytest via the main worktree's interpreter rather than creating a per-worktree venv. **Why:** the venv holds multi-GB ML deps (torch/faiss/transformers); duplicating per worktree costs minutes + disk with no benefit. Code resolves from each worktree's own cwd, so isolation is preserved. Verified green from `cc2-backend`.

## 2026-07-17 · Permanent knowledge base committed to `main`
`CLAUDE.md` + `.claude/{skills,commands,project}` committed before worktrees branch off, then worktrees fast-forwarded. **Why:** worktrees check out the branch tip; the base must exist first so every session starts with full context.

## 2026-07-16 · No Claude/AI attribution in git
No `Co-Authored-By: Claude` or "Generated with Claude Code" in commits/PRs; author is the user only. **Why:** explicit user preference; commits were rewritten + force-pushed to strip trailers.

## 2026-07-16 · Generic skill semantics replace hardcoded lookups
`skill_semantics_service.py` classifies categories by nearest-centroid over `TECH_TAXONOMY` embeddings and estimates transfer by cosine similarity + same-category boost. **Why:** the mandate is "algorithms not examples" — the old `CATEGORY_TAXONOMY` dict and `if req in [...] and has_python` chains didn't generalize to unseen skills.

## 2026-07-16 · Two-agent runtime, LangGraph sequential
Exactly two runtime agents (Candidate Intelligence → Hiring Decision), sequential graph, no branching. **Why:** mirrors real hiring (gather evidence, then decide), keeps every decision explainable; extra agents add complexity without value.

## 2026-08-27 · Company intelligence is a separate module, not a third agent
Employer/company records live in their own package (`services/company/*`), their own store (Qdrant Cloud), their own router (`/api/v1/company-chat`) and their own UI mode. Nothing in `services/ai/` imports it and the LangGraph workflow is untouched. **Why:** the mandate was "don't change anything existing working". Company records answer a different question against a different corpus with a different notion of relevance; folding them into `chat_service.answer` would put two unrelated populations behind one ranking. The two-runtime-agent rule is intact — like the recruiter chatbot, this is a service, not an agent.

## 2026-08-27 · One point per company FIELD, whole row in every payload
Each company becomes ~7 Qdrant points (about, clients, services, products, culture, ceo_details, industries), and every point carries the entire row in its payload. **Why:** embedding seven unrelated paragraphs as one blob averages five topics into one vector, so a culture question competes against product names and loses. Carrying the whole row costs nothing at this scale and means a follow-up about another field needs no second round trip — and that a column not embedded today is still answerable.

## 2026-08-27 · A column earns a vector by reading like prose, not by being on a list
`excel_loader` embeds a column when its median value length clears a threshold, so an unknown `Tech Stack` column becomes searchable automatically while `Founded Year` stays payload-only. **Why:** Golden Rule 4. The sheet's schema is still moving; a maintained list of blessed headers would need editing on every change, and embedding "1968" puts a number into a semantic space where it means nothing.

## 2026-08-27 · A rare word in a company name identifies it; a common one does not
`find_companies` accepts a single overlapping word when that word is rare across the corpus, measured from the indexed text. **Why:** requiring a fixed share of the name silently failed "what products does TCS build" — "tcs" is one of three name words but appears in exactly one company's prose, making it a far stronger identifier than two generic ones. Same principle as `chat_lexicon`: the pool decides what its own words mean.

## 2026-08-27 · Company embeddings hard-fail instead of falling back to a local model
`embedding_engines.gpu_engine_strict()` raises rather than degrading to BGE. **Why:** `resolve_engine`'s fallback is right for FAISS (a slightly worse answer beats no answer) and wrong for a fixed-dimension external store — writing 384-dim vectors into a 768-dim Qdrant collection is either rejected or, worse, quietly searches the wrong semantic space.

## 2026-08-27 · People get a vector of their own, inside the same collection
Each person becomes one point with `field="person"`, embedding "<name> at <company> — role: …", while the full people table stays in every company payload. **Why:** payload-only people (the original decision) made a person reachable ONLY by typing their name exactly — "who leads engineering at a fintech company" was unanswerable, because a payload table cannot be searched by meaning. This adds a searchable copy without moving people out of the company record or creating a second collection. Point ids are keyed on the person's name, so reordering the sheet does not clone anyone.

## 2026-08-27 · The resume SCOPE guardrail is the only verdict company mode overrides
Injection, secrets, fairness and abuse detection are reused verbatim from `chat_guardrails`; only SCOPE is ignored, and only the refusal wording is re-written. **Why:** SCOPE encodes "this pool is resumes", true for the candidate chatbot and false here. Everything else transfers unchanged — an injection attempt is an injection attempt whichever corpus it targets.
